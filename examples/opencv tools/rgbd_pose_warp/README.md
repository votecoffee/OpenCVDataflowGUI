# RGB-D pose warp

Open [the project](rgbd_pose_warp.json) and run the saved recording once.
**Warp RGB-D by camera pose** transforms the color and depth views into
the new optical frame and shows the warped color. Review **Depth** for
destination camera Z, **Valid mask** for coverage, and **Source index** for
the winning source pixel. A source index of −1 marks a hole.

The source frame has a foreground at 1 m and a background at 2 m.
The pose adds 0.75 m to camera X and 1 m to camera Z, so the destination depths
are 2 m and 3 m. At 20 destination cells both surfaces compete. The nearer
surface supplies both depth and color at every one of those cells.

The replay stores color and depth in separate PNG files on the same known
pixel grid. **Assume registered** declares that alignment to the block.
In a graph with independently calibrated cameras, register the RGB-D before
applying the pose warp. The output uses the original intrinsics and size
under a new optical frame. OpenCV's floor rasterization can leave holes
where the projected samples do not cover the target grid.
[OpenCV `warpFrame` reference](https://docs.opencv.org/5.0/main_modules/ptcloud_rgbd.html#warpframe)

The synthetic recording was generated by the project contributors and is
covered by the repository's MIT license. Use `build_example.py --output <empty-folder>`
to regenerate the project and recording.

```powershell
opencv-dataflow-run "examples/opencv tools/rgbd_pose_warp/rgbd_pose_warp.json" --profile lean
```
